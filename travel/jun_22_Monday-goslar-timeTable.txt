Easy Snack Strategy (No Sitting Down)
--
Since you want to keep moving, look for these as you walk from the station to the Market:
The "Goslarer Adler" Bakery: Pick up a "Harzer Schmorwurst" (a local spicy sausage) in a bread roll, 
or a slice of Harzer Käse (local cheese) if you're feeling adventurous.

Fish Rolls (Fischbrötchen): There is often a stand near the Market Square selling 
fresh rolls with herring or salmon—very traditional for a quick North German lunch.

Street Stall Bratwurst: On the Schuhhof (the oldest square in town, just behind the Market), 
there is almost always a grill going.

The Slate Houses: Look closely at the buildings; Goslar is unique because many houses are entirely covered in blue-grey 
slate tiles to protect them from the Harz Mountains' weather.

Monday is a great day to visit because it’s much quieter than the weekend. However, the Glockenspiel is popular, 
so if you are in the Market Square at 15:00, stop for 5 minutes to watch the show.

For a quick, high-quality "grab-and-go" coffee before you hop back on the RE train to Hannover, 
you have two great options depending on your walking route back to the station:

1. Baristokat (The "Local Craft" Choice)
  This is widely considered the best specialty coffee in Goslar. 
  It’s located on Rosentorstraße 4, which is the main pedestrian street (about 5-6 minutes from the station).
  you will walk down to get from the Old Town back to the train station.

2. Bäckerei & Konditorei Müller (The "Traditional" Choice)
  Location: Rosentorstraße 22 (even closer to the station, about a 3-minute walk).
  If you want a coffee and perhaps one last local pastry (like a Harzer Roller or a piece of plum cake) 
  to eat on the train, this traditional bakery is ideal.
